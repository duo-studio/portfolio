Duo Studio concept notes

A legacy institution needs clarity before decoration.

This homepage concept respects the museum's seriousness while making the first screen more useful: what is on view, how to visit, how to learn, and how to support the institution.

Hero strategy

Lead with legacy, then give people a next step.

The design keeps the museum's black, gold, red, and cream world intact. The visual system is quiet, confident, and institutional, with strong typography and measured structure instead of spectacle.

1

The homepage opens like a front desk.

Visitors immediately see what The Lewis is, where it is, when it is open, how much admission costs, and which next action makes sense.

2

TITAN becomes the emotional anchor.

The current exhibition gives the first view specificity and honors Reginald F. Lewis without making the homepage feel like a generic institutional brochure.

3

The audience paths stay useful, not oversized.

Visit, exhibitions, field trips, and support are presented as clear choices with enough context to reduce hesitation, but not so much that the page becomes a wall of cards.

4

Education and rentals move into view.

Field trips and event rentals are meaningful revenue and mission paths, so the concept makes them visible without letting them compete with the core museum visit.

5

Mobile preserves the same order.

The mobile page does not simply shrink the desktop composition. It keeps logo, hours, location, the thesis, the current exhibition, and the primary paths in a readable stack.

The tone is legacy-forward, not nostalgic.

The concept treats The Lewis as a living institution: rooted in history, active in education, and central to Baltimore's civic and cultural life.
